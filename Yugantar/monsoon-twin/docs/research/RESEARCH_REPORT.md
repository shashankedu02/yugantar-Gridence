# Build a small, local, honest monsoon twin

The strongest realistic Track 2 entry for a beginner team in 24 hours is not a transformer or a Fourier model. It is a **small residual CNN with a deliberately limited receptive field (about 8 cells per daily step)**. It predicts day-to-day changes in climatology-anchored anomalies. It sits on a damped-persistence fallback, gets 2–4 step rollout fine-tuning, and is finished with cheap per-lead post-processing for variance and heavy rain. The official kit makes this choice unusually clear. Its scorer gives **full RMSE points at only 12% mean skill over the better of persistence and climatology**, and full CSI points at a CSI skill of 0.10. Anti-blur credit rises steadily up to 40% of the true day-8 spatial variance and drops to zero above 250%. Shock causality is scored by a day-1 locality test and an 8-cells-per-day "teleportation" radius, so any layer that mixes information across the whole domain (FFT, global attention, a deep U-Net) is a structural liability. The model runs offline on 4 CPU cores with 6 GB RAM, gets 5 s per 10-step rollout, and is scored on only 5 hidden 2023–24 windows plus 3 shocks. A model of a few hundred thousand parameters is enough and safer. Blur and heavy rain are best handled after training: rescale anomalies per lead, and calibrate rainfall amounts around the 10 mm/day threshold. Retraining with exotic losses buys little here. The dashboard should be a plain HTML/Canvas app with vendored libraries, running live off `/simulate`, and it should show the same locality and propagation diagnostics the scorer computes. The impact module is most defensible when built around the Rajeevan et al. monsoon break index and IMD rainfall-departure bands, presented as proxies with explicit caveats.

*How to read this report.* Each section separates **Cited evidence**, which is facts from the sources linked inline, from **Inference**, which is our reasoning, arithmetic and design recommendations and is not reported results. Some kit details were read directly from the official repository and track PDF while this report was being written; the notes had left them open. These include the scorer's exact formulas, the repo-scan rules, the round 2 gate and the channel standard deviations, and they are cited to the specific kit file.

## The official kit pins down every scored behaviour, and the bar is lower than it looks

**Cited evidence.** The team kit is public at `OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit`. It has a single commit tagged v1.0.0 and was published around 03:03 IST on 26 Nov 2026. Its Release serves `india_climate_train.nc` (122,831,709 bytes), `stats.json`, the kit wheel and an `API_CONTRACT.md`. That last file is **byte-identical to `docs/R1_CONTRACT.md`**, which explains why the PDF and the repo use different names for it ([kit repo](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit); [API_CONTRACT.md asset](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/releases/download/v1.0.0/API_CONTRACT.md)). The grid is 32 × 36 cells of 1°, with centres at 6.5–37.5°N and 65.5–100.5°E. The channels, in physical units on the wire, are t2m (K), tp (mm/day), u10 and v10 (m/s), msl (Pa) and tcwv (kg/m²). Daily values are means of the 00/06/12/18 UTC samples, except tp, which is the 24 h sum. `doy_sincos` refers to the *last* input day, and calendar years are never sent ([R1_CONTRACT.md](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/docs/R1_CONTRACT.md); [contract.py](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/monsoon_r1/contract.py)). The training file covers 2005–2021 and includes two static channels, `land_sea_mask` and `orography_m`, which teams must bundle with their model ([README](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/README.md)). `stats.json` gives domain-wide per-channel standard deviations of **13.46 K, 7.55 mm/day, 2.90 m/s, 2.43 m/s, 619 Pa and 20.49 kg/m²** ([stats.json](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/releases/download/v1.0.0/stats.json)).

The judge runs the repo offline with `python serve.py --port 8000` on **6 GB RAM, 4 CPU cores and no GPU**, on a read-only filesystem. Available libraries are torch (CPU), numpy, scipy, pandas, xarray, scikit-learn, lightgbm, onnxruntime, FastAPI, Flask and uvicorn. It polls `POST /health` every 2 s for up to 120 s, then makes 8 `/simulate` calls with a 5.0 s limit each and no retries. Five calls are real windows dated after 2023-01-10, and the track PDF describes them as covering **monsoon onset, heavy rain, heatwave, cyclone and winter disturbance**. Three are shocks on one real window: `warm_ocean` (+3 K t2m and +15% tcwv over the Bay of Bengal box, 8–20°N, 82–94°E), `monsoon_stall` (−40% u10 and v10 over the Arabian Sea box, 8–20°N, 65–75°E) and `null` (zeros) ([R1_CONTRACT.md](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/docs/R1_CONTRACT.md); Track 2 PDF, team-provided). To reach the human-judged desk review, a team needs **valid outputs on all 8 calls, a win over persistence at day 4, and a non-zero localised response to at least one shock**. The top 10 qualifiers then demo live, judges apply a new shock on the spot, and the top 2 go to the finals ([R1_CONTRACT.md](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/docs/R1_CONTRACT.md); Track 2 PDF).

The scorer's constants come straight from the code ([scoring.py](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/monsoon_r1/scoring.py)):

| Component | Points | Exact rule in the kit |
|---|---|---|
| RMSE skill | 30 | Lat-weighted (cos φ, unit mean) RMSE per channel; SS = 1 − RMSE / min(RMSE_persistence, RMSE_climatology); points = 30 × clip(mean SS / **0.12**, 0, 1) |
| Heavy-rain CSI | 15 | CSI at tp > **10 mm/day**; skill = (CSI − ref)/(1 − ref), ref = better of persistence/climatology CSI; points = 15 × clip(mean skill / **0.10**, 0, 1) |
| Anti-blur | 10 | Day-8 spatial-variance ratio per channel, averaged over windows; channel credit = clip(r / 0.40, 0, 1), zeroed if r > 2.5 |
| Causality | 15 | Per shock, quarters for: non-zero (max \|δ\|/std ≥ 1e-3); localised (≥ 60% of day-1 energy within the box dilated by 3 cells); propagates (mean energy outside that neighbourhood on days 4–8 > 1.5× day 1 and > 2% of peak, *and* < 5% of energy beyond 3 + 8·day cells on every day); stable (max \|δ\| ≤ 5 std). Propagation and stability count only if localised. A failed null test halves the total |
| Repo scan | 15 | Anti-cheat 8 + code health 7 (see below) |
| Brief | 15 | Two pages: model, drift/blur prevention and failure modes; then visualisation and impact |

The validity gate rejects NaN/Inf, any tp below −1e-6, and t2m outside 233.15–333.15 K ([scoring.py](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/monsoon_r1/scoring.py)). The repo scanner never executes code. It treats the file that serves `/simulate`, plus every local module it imports, as "inference code". A **critical** flag wipes out all 8 anti-cheat points. Critical flags are raised for: any external weather or data string in inference code (including `weatherbench2`, `gcp-public-data-arco-era5`, `cdsapi`, `imdlib`, `open-meteo`); five or more date literals in inference code; any `.npy`/`.npz` whose first dimension exceeds 400 with trailing shape (32, 36), so a 366-day climatology is explicitly allowed; and the absence of a weights file over 1 KB. A `.nc`/`.grib`/`.zarr` file anywhere in the repo, or 3–4 date literals in inference code, costs 2 points per warning. Code health awards 3 points for a training file calling `.backward(`/`optimizer.step`, 2 for keeping streamlit/gradio/plotly/matplotlib out of inference files, 1 for a frontend (`index.html`, `app.js` and so on), 0.5 for a README and 0.5 for a checkpoint ([repo_scan.py](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/monsoon_r1/repo_scan.py)). The shipped `stats.py` builds the reference climatology as a calendar-day mean with a **15-day circular running mean**, in 366 slots ([stats.py](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/monsoon_r1/stats.py)). The kit's builder notes that WeatherBench 2's ERA5 stops on 2023-01-10, so later dates come from ARCO-ERA5 at 0.25° averaged to the 1° grid ([build_dataset.py](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/monsoon_r1/build_dataset.py)). Both facts are confirmed by the WB2 data guide and the ARCO README ([WB2 data guide](https://weatherbench2.readthedocs.io/en/latest/data-guide.html); [ARCO-ERA5](https://github.com/google-research/arco-era5)).

On the event itself, the only listing found is an aggregator. It names **Scaler School of Technology, Bengaluru** as organiser, lists ₹50,000 in prizes (₹25k/15k/10k) and teams of 2–4, and says all code must be written during the hackathon ([StartupGrantsIndia](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/competitions/the-devforge-hackathon)). Its description of round 1 as a design document does not match the Track 2 kit. The track PDF's "Original work … built during the hackathon" rule agrees with the aggregator. ISRO's BAH 2026 did include "an AI-powered digital twin of India's climate" among 15 problem statements, but its number "PS-5" could not be confirmed from a fetched source ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/education/story/isro-bharatiya-antariksh-hackathon-2026-how-to-apply-eligibility-more-536547-2026-06-12); [ISRO BAH 2026](https://www.isro.gov.in/Bharatiya_Antariksh_Hackathon_2026_Grand_Finale.html)). The kit's NOTICE attributes the data as modified Copernicus/ERA5 under CC BY 4.0. The CC BY 4.0 status was not confirmed from an ECMWF primary page ([NOTICE](https://github.com/OfficialAbhinavSingh/yug-monsoon-kit/blob/main/NOTICE)).

**Inference.** Three things follow. First, **RMSE skill saturates at 0.12**. A model averaging, say, 0.30 at day 1, 0.06 at day 4 and 0.00 at day 8 already earns all 30 points, if the grader averages over leads and channels; the shipped code does not show how per-window SS is aggregated. Any surplus skill is therefore better spent on variance and rain calibration than on chasing more RMSE. Second, the scorer is deliberately lenient in some places and strict in others. Five std is about 67 K for t2m, so the stability check bites only on true blow-ups. The validity gate's t2m and tp bounds are what actually need clamping. The day-1 teleport radius is 11 cells from the box edge and 19 cells by day 2, so on a 32 × 36 grid the teleport test effectively constrains only days 1–2. Third, the repo scan is a minefield for innocent mistakes. A comment saying "built from weatherbench2", a docstring listing "2005-01-01 to 2021-12-31", a `data/india_climate_train.nc` left in the tree, or a dashboard helper that happens to contain the string `/simulate` and imports plotly can each cost points. Keep attribution text in the README only, keep dates out of `serve.py` and anything it imports, and `.gitignore` all data. Only 5 windows are scored, so **variance between windows is huge**. One failed window scores zero, and there is no way to tune to the test set, which argues for robust, regime-agnostic choices over leaderboard-style tuning. Ask the organisers' WhatsApp channel two questions: how SS is averaged across leads and channels, and whether a few example initial states may ship with the dashboard.

## A local residual CNN on anomalies beats bigger architectures in this setting

**Cited evidence.** The global state-of-the-art models were built for problems that bear little resemblance to this one. FourCastNet's AFNO mixes tokens through a 2D FFT on a 720 × 1440 grid, trained in about 16 h on 64 A100s ([Pathak et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11214)). SFNO's authors note that the FFT "incorrectly assume[s] a flat geometry" with wrong periodicity, and they train for hours on 8 DGX machines ([Bonev et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03838)). GraphCast has 36.7M parameters and trained for about 4 weeks on 32 TPU v4s ([Lam et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.12794)). Stormer, a ViT-L-scale model, needed under 24 h on 128 A100s ([Nguyen et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.03876)). At the small end, the WeatherBench CNN has **313,858 parameters** ([Rasp et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.00469)) and the DLWP cubed-sphere U-Net about **700,000** ([Weyn et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.11927)). Rasp & Thuerey's 19-block, 128-channel ResNet hit a **data-limited ceiling**: larger networks overfit more, and only pretraining on 150 years of CMIP output relieved it ([Rasp & Thuerey 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.08626)). Naive iteration is dangerous. The WeatherBench iterative CNN "performs well up to about 1.5 days, then its errors diverge", ending worse than persistence ([Rasp et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.00469)). DLWP, by contrast, stayed stable over 210 four-week forecasts and a 1-year free run, which its authors partly credit to a capped leaky ReLU ([Weyn et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.11927)).

The training recipe is consistent across the literature. GraphCast predicts a **difference added to the last state**, scaled by the standard deviation of time differences, with latitude- and variable-weighted MSE ([Lam et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.12794)). Stormer's ablation shows that predicting dynamics beats predicting absolute states ([Nguyen et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.03876)). Everyone pretrains on one step and then fine-tunes on short rollouts. FourCastNet uses 2 steps at LR 1e-4 ([Pathak et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11214)). Neural-LAM uses 4 steps and found longer rollouts gave only "minor improvements" ([Oskarsson et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17370)). Stormer runs K = 1 → 4 → 8 at falling LRs ([Nguyen et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.03876)). Time-of-year forcing and static fields help: DLWP reports that adding insolation, land-sea mask and topography "improved validation performance" ([Weyn et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.11927)). Precipitation is log-transformed in both FourCastNet and Rasp & Thuerey. Regional ML models such as Neural-LAM **assume boundary forcing** at each step, overwriting a 10-cell band, and nobody has tested running them without it ([Oskarsson et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17370)). Realistic expectations are modest. At 5.625° globally, the best ResNet roughly halves persistence error for T850 at day 3, but beats climatology for precipitation by only about 8% ([Rasp & Thuerey 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.08626)). DLWP's T2 RMSE stays below climatology only until about day 8 ([Weyn et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.11927)).

**Inference.** On a bounded 32 × 36 box with about 6,200 daily samples, global-mixing architectures are a poor fit for three reasons. They wrap the east edge onto the west. Their training budgets are orders of magnitude too large. And, as the next sections show, they break the scorer's locality test by construction. The recommended design is an **anomaly-residual local CNN**:

| Element | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Inputs | 3 days × 6 channels as standardized anomalies from the day-of-year climatology (tp in log1p space), plus the target-day climatology, 2 statics, doy sin/cos broadcast (~28 channels) | Climatology is the day-8 reference; anomalies make "revert to climatology" easy to learn |
| Output | 6-channel increment of the anomaly for the next day | Tendency prediction; zero output = persistence of anomaly |
| Body | ~8 blocks of [3×3 conv → GELU → 1×1 conv] with residual skips, 64–96 channels, no InstanceNorm/LayerNorm over space | ~0.3–0.6M params; per-step receptive-field radius = 8 cells |
| Padding | Zero padding in anomaly space | Equivalent to "climatology outside the domain", a cheap pseudo-boundary |
| Loss | cos-lat-weighted MSE, per-channel weights | Matches the scorer's metric |
| Schedule | One-step training to convergence (~30–50 epochs, AdamW, weight decay), then 2–4-step rollout fine-tune at 10× lower LR | The cheap form of the literature curriculum |
| Split | Train 2005–2018, validate 2019–2021, stratified by season | Mirrors the post-2023 hidden test |
| Fallback | Damped anomaly persistence, x̂ₖ = clim + φ_{c,k}·(x₀ − clim₀), φ fitted per channel and lead | Ships in hour 2; stable by construction |

Rough FLOP arithmetic: a 3×3 conv with 64→64 channels on 1,152 cells costs about 85 MFLOP. The design above is therefore around 1 GFLOP per step and about 10 GFLOP per rollout, which should run well under 1 s on 4 cores. A full Rasp-size ResNet (about 13 GFLOP per step) would push toward the 5 s limit. The day-of-year index can be recovered as atan2(sin, cos)·365/2π. Use the kit's own `stats.climatology()` so that your anchor matches the grader's reference exactly. It is 366 × 6 × 32 × 36 float32, about 10 MB, and is below the scanner's 400-row archive threshold. Ensembling 2–3 seeds lowers RMSE cheaply but adds blur, so pair it with the rescaling described below. LightGBM per cell is possible but awkward to roll out autoregressively. ConvLSTM adds complexity that stacking the 3 days as channels already covers. The fallback is valuable for more than safety. A pointwise model scores the non-zero, localised and stable quarters on both shocks (its outside-neighbourhood energy never grows), which is about **11.25 of 15 causality points**. It should also clear the day-4 persistence gate comfortably, because persistence degrades quickly by day 4. Expected skill, our guess and not a reported result: meaningful at day 1 for t2m, msl and tcwv; small at day 4; near zero at day 8, where the goal is simply not to lose to climatology.

## Blur and heavy rain are calibration problems, not architecture problems

**Cited evidence.** Blur is a mathematical consequence of MSE training. For a partly predictable target, the MSE-optimal forecast has smaller amplitude than the truth, and misplaced features are penalised twice ([Subich et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19374v2)). WeatherBench 2 calls blurry forecasts "a direct consequence of these models minimizing a deterministic mean error". These forecasts score well on RMSE but "do not represent a possible weather state". For precipitation, WB2 reports that RMSE "heavily favor[s] unrealistically smooth forecasts", and the model ranking on SEEPS is the reverse of the ranking on RMSE ([Rasp et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.15560)). Longer rollout training makes GraphCast blurrier at long leads ([Lam et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.12794)), and DLWP found that large rollout horizons produced "overly smooth forecasts tending toward climatology" ([Weyn et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.11927)). The heavyweight fixes are expensive. AMSE fine-tuning of GraphCast cost about 26 A100 GPU-days and needs spherical harmonics; its limited-area use is untested ([Subich et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19374v2)). FGN's CRPS ensembles cost about 490 TPU-days per model ([Alet et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10772v1)). AIFS-CRPS keeps "realistic variability" only through stochastic multi-member training ([Lang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15832v1)). An ensemble mean is itself blurry, so only a single member helps a variance metric ([Rasp et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.15560)).

For rainfall, PostRainBench's class-weighted cross-entropy head was decisive. Removing it cut heavy-rain CSI from **0.209 to 0.042** in Germany ([PostRainBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02676v3)). Frequency-matching calibration reduces frequency bias and raises ETS, but gains are "limited" at high thresholds. When pattern errors dominate, it trades bias for random error, and a dry bias "can never be removed" ([Zhu & Luo 2015](https://www.emc.ncep.noaa.gov/gmb/yzhu/gif/pub/WAF2015_Zhu_Luo.pdf)). AI models over the Indian monsoon underrepresent days above 50 mm/day and overpredict light-to-moderate rain ([MAUSAM evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.01879)). For context, IMD's "heavy rain" begins at 64.5 mm/day at station scale ([IMD 2020](https://internal.imd.gov.in/press_release/20201124_pr_932.pdf)). The 10 mm/day threshold therefore sits in IMD's moderate band ([IMD Pune glossary](https://imdpune.gov.in:443/Reports/glossary.pdf)), which suits 1° daily grid means. ERA5 does not assimilate Indian gauges, and it runs about 9% wetter than IMD nationally and 56% wetter in the Brahmaputra basin ([Goteti & Famiglietti 2024](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/3435/2024/)). Since ERA5 is the truth here, match the ERA5 distribution, not IMD's.

**Inference.** For a standardized anomaly with forecast-truth correlation r, the MSE-optimal anomaly variance is r² of the truth's. At r ≈ 0.4 on day 8, only about 16% of anomaly variance survives. Inflating fully to 100% raises MSE from 1 − r² to 2(1 − r), which at r = 0.5 exactly ties climatology. The scorer, however, measures **total-field** spatial variance, and that includes the climatological pattern. Channels with strong fixed gradients (t2m north–south and land–sea, msl monsoon trough, tcwv moist south and dry northwest) will probably clear 40% even when heavily blurred. **tp, then u10/v10, are the at-risk channels.** The cheapest robust remedy is a post-hoc per-channel, per-lead factor: x′ = clim + a_{c,L}·(x − clim). Fit a on validation so that the day-8 ratio sits around 0.6–0.8, not 1.0, and accept a change only if RMSE skill stays above about 0.12. Watch one trap closely. The anti-blur ratio is averaged across windows *before* clipping, and the hidden set includes a pre-monsoon heatwave window that will be nearly dry. A tp field with spurious drizzle patterns in such a window could post a ratio of 10 or more, drag the mean above 2.5, and **zero the entire tp channel**. Make tp inflation conditional on predicted wetness, and check ratios by season. Log the variance ratio for every channel and lead each epoch, together with a Hann-windowed 2D spectrum, which has only about 16 radial bins on this grid.

For heavy rain, the scorer thresholds your single tp field at 10 mm/day, so any exceedance head must be folded back into tp. The simplest version that works is three steps. Train tp in log1p space. Then, per lead, fit a monotone amount remapping, either quantile mapping against training-truth ERA5 or a single multiplicative stretch for wet cells, that maximises validation CSI at 10 mm/day. Expect it to over-forecast slightly, with frequency bias above 1. Finally, verify that tp RMSE still beats both references. A weighted-BCE exceedance head, used to push cells just over or just under 10, is the next step up and changes few cells, so it costs little RMSE. Compute persistence and climatology CSI on your own validation data. In the monsoon, persistence is a strong reference at day 1, and over the Western Ghats and northeast India the July–August climatological mean itself can exceed 10 mm/day, which makes the day-8 reference non-trivial. GANs, diffusion models and CRPS ensembles are not worth the risk in 24 hours: a single stochastic member usually has higher RMSE, which threatens the score that matters most.

## Shock causality is decided by receptive field and determinism

**Cited evidence.** Localized-perturbation tests on Pangu-Weather produced physically recognisable responses. These include a Matsuno-Gill pattern near tropical heating, geostrophic adjustment in about 6 h, tropical gravity signals at about 20 m/s, and hurricanes that develop only above about 5 hPa initial amplitude and never when initial humidity is zero ([Hakim & Masanam, arXiv:2309.10867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10867); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.10867)). Pangu smooths small scales away within 24 h, with an effective resolution of about 500 km. It "completely fails" to reproduce the rapid growth of tiny perturbations, and its spread pattern barely depends on perturbation amplitude ([Selz & Craig 2023](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/110134/1/Geophysical_Research_Letters_-_2023_-_Selz_-_Can_Artificial_Intelligence%E2%80%90Based_Weather_Prediction_Models_Simulate_the__1_.pdf)). A CNN's effective receptive field is Gaussian and "only occupies a fraction of the full theoretical receptive field" ([Luo et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04128)). Dirac-delta tests on neural operators find that spatial-domain (convolutional) models learn local dependencies better than function-space/global ones ([Gao et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02683v1)). Physical speeds bracket the scorer's 8 cells/day (about 10 m/s): equatorial Rossby waves move at about 4.5 m/s, the MJO at 4–8 m/s, convectively coupled Kelvin waves at 12–15 m/s over the Indian Ocean, and dry gravity waves at 44–53 m/s ([Kiladis et al. 2009](https://www.meteo.physik.uni-muenchen.de/~roger/Lectures/TropicalMetweb/Literature/RevGeoph_2009_Kiladis_etal.pdf)). On the expected physics, many monsoon lows that rain on central India form in the northwest Bay of Bengal, and a warm coastal SST front "helps to sustain deep and intense convection" ([Samanta et al. 2018](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-018-35735-3)). Monsoon breaks feature weaker westerlies over southern India and negative rainfall anomalies over the Western Ghats ([Rai et al., accepted manuscript](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/64658/1/CLDY_D_16_00950_R1acceptedversion.pdf)). In the transition to a break, Arabian Sea moisture at 850 hPa "diminishes rapidly" ([Annamalai & Sperber 2004](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/859923)). Ensembles from initial conditions alone are underdispersive and cannot represent model error ([Bülte et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.13458); [Mahesh et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2408.03100v3)).

**Inference.** A stride-1 CNN with L layers of 3×3 kernels can move information at most R = L cells per step. If R ≤ 8 per daily step, the scorer's teleport test (energy beyond 3 + 8·day cells) **cannot fail by construction**, whatever the training does. That is the strongest argument for the architecture above. A U-Net with two or three poolings on 32 × 36 has a whole-domain receptive field. So do FFT layers, global attention, squeeze-excitation and instance or layer normalisation over space. Each lets a Bay of Bengal shock nudge Rajasthan on day 1. Avoid them, or run BatchNorm strictly in eval mode. Too small a radius is also a risk. With R ≈ 2, energy may never leave the 3-cell neighbourhood by days 4–8, and the propagation quarter fails. An R of 6–8 with most weight in the inner cells fits the physics and the scorer.

Energy is measured in domain-wide std units, which gives some rough magnitudes. `monsoon_stall` on monsoon-season westerlies of about 8 m/s (a background-knowledge value) gives roughly (3.2/2.90)² ≈ 1.2 std² per cell, so wind dominates. `warm_ocean` gives only about 0.05 from t2m and about 0.13–0.19 from tcwv per cell, so its downstream response is likely to show up mainly in tp and the winds, and it risks fading out. The Selz & Craig evidence suggests damping, not explosion, is the realistic failure. Check that the day-4–8 energy outside the neighbourhood really exceeds 1.5× day 1. The Arabian Sea box sits on the western domain edge, at columns 0–9, so watch for energy piling up against the wall. One subtle hazard: the shock is added only to the third input day, so a tendency model sees an abrupt jump of +3 K or −40% between days 2 and 3 and may extrapolate it. Training with random localized perturbations added to the last input day is a legitimate fix. It teaches the network that such jumps exist without changing the contract.

For physically sensible signs in the brief and the demo, expect `warm_ocean` to bring lower msl, more tp and tcwv, and cyclonic, convergent winds over and west or north of the box on day 1, spreading northwest toward Odisha and the monsoon trough. Expect `monsoon_stall` to bring drying over the Kerala–Konkan coast and Western Ghats, 0–3 cells east of the box, spreading east like a monsoon break, with part of the wind deficit recovering because the pressure field was untouched. Rising msl over the warm box, or no msl change at all after a large wind cut, are red flags that the channels are not coupled. For the null test, keep inference deterministic: eval mode, no dropout, float32 throughout. Make sure the `perturbation is None` and all-zeros code paths are numerically identical. Keep any ensemble noise out of the scored path entirely.

## The dashboard and impact module win the human rounds

**Cited evidence.** The PS requires a dashboard and impact module "driven live" by your own `/simulate`, never pre-rendered. The dashboard code must be separate from the server. Its examples include crop stress, solar/wind generation, heatwave or flood risk, ensemble uncertainty and a policy co-pilot, and judges apply a new shock live (Track 2 PDF). Every candidate library is permissively licensed and can be vendored: Leaflet (BSD-2), D3 (ISC), Plotly.js (MIT), ECharts (Apache-2.0) and deck.gl (MIT) ([jsDelivr Leaflet](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/leaflet@1.9.4/package.json); [D3](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/d3/package.json); [Plotly](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/plotly.js-dist-min/package.json); [ECharts](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/echarts@5/package.json); [deck.gl](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/deck.gl/package.json)). cambecc/earth (MIT) shows that 1° grids drawn on HTML5 Canvas over vector boundaries, with bilinear interpolation, work without tiles ([earth](https://github.com/cambecc/earth)). Natural Earth is public domain and publishes an **India point-of-view** boundary file ([terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/); [POV files](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/?p=5118)). Survey of India certifies India's external boundary on published maps, and maps without the boundary or coastline need no scrutiny ([SOI](https://surveyofindia.gov.in/pages/scrutiny-of-external-boundary-and-coast-line-of-india)). Rainbow colour maps distort data by more than 7% of the displayed variation, and about 8% of men have a colour-vision deficiency. Use perceptually uniform maps, diverging maps for anomalies, and always show a colour bar ([Crameri et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7595127); [cmocean, Thyng et al. 2016](https://tos.org/oceanography/article/true-colors-of-oceanography-guidelines-for-effective-and-accurate-colormap)). Climate Pulse pairs a map with time series against an explicit baseline, gives hover values and CSV export, and labels data latency ([Copernicus](https://climate.copernicus.eu/climate-pulse-tool-take-temperature-our-planet-glance)). FastAPI's CORS middleware treats protocol + host + port as the origin, and allows only GET by default ([FastAPI CORS](https://fastapi.tiangolo.com/tutorial/cors/)).

The impact indicators rest on official definitions. IMD heatwaves need **station maximum** temperature (≥ 40°C in the plains, plus a departure or absolute rule, at at least 2 stations on 2 consecutive days) ([IMD definitions](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/pdfs/heatcolduser/Definition.pdf)), and a 2026 report says these parameters may change ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/climate-change-crisis-imd-to-change-parameters-to-declare-heatwaves-126051600450_1.html)). The heat index needs relative humidity ([NOAA WPC](https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/html/heatindex_equation.shtml)). IMD rainfall-departure bands run from Large Excess (≥ 60%) through Normal (−19% to +19%) to Large Deficient (−60% to −99%) ([IMD legend](https://mausam.imd.gov.in/Rainfall/STATE_RAINFALL_DISTRIBUTION_COUNTRY_INDIA_cd.pdf)). Rajeevan et al. define a July–August **break** as a normalized rainfall anomaly over the monsoon core zone (about 18–28°N, 65–88°E) below −1.0 for three or more consecutive days, and an active spell as above +1.0 ([IITM ERPAS](https://www.tropmet.res.in/erpas/files/active_break_selection.php); [Rajeevan et al. 2010](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s12040-010-0019-4)). Power-law wind extrapolation with α ≈ 1/7 is generally acceptable only for height differences under about 50 m ([wind profile power law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_profile_power_law)).

**Inference.** Build the dashboard as a static `dashboard/` folder: `index.html` plus vendored JS and fonts, served with `python -m http.server 8080`. Add `CORSMiddleware` to `serve.py` listing both `localhost:8080` and `127.0.0.1:8080`, with POST allowed. Do not write a Python dashboard server that contains the string `/simulate`, because the scanner would treat it as inference code. Draw the 32 × 36 field as a scaled Canvas `ImageData` through a 256-entry colour lookup table. Keep the colour-bar ranges fixed across baseline, perturbed and time steps. Use cmocean-style sequential maps for fields and symmetric diverging maps for differences. Overlay a heavily simplified India POV boundary with an "indicative, not SOI-certified" note, and write a roughly 150-line Canvas particle advector for u10/v10, with a "grid arrows" toggle so judges can see the real 1° resolution. The centrepiece for the live round is a **shock painter**: brush a box or Gaussian on the map, pick channel and amplitude, run both baseline and shocked rollouts, and show a Baseline | Perturbed | Difference triptych with a day scrubber. Then add the panel no other team will have: **energy versus distance from the shock, per day, with the scorer's 3-cell and 3 + 8·day radii drawn in**. It turns the causality test into a visible scientific argument. For demo initial states, ship a handful of example 3-day windows as small JSON files in the dashboard folder, not in inference code, and confirm with the organisers that this does not count as an "archive".

For impact, make one indicator the flagship and tie it to the shocks. A **"monsoon break and kharif rain-deficit" module** fits best. It computes the Rajeevan core-zone index from the emulator's tp (normalized with the emulator's own July–August climatology), flags break and active runs, and reports the 10-day rainfall departure in IMD bands, both against climatology and as a shocked-versus-baseline change. `monsoon_stall` then produces a visibly break-like signal that judges can test live. Add secondary cards with explicit "computed as" formulas: grid-mean rainfall in IMD intensity bands; 3- and 5-day accumulations labelled "flood-hazard proxy", not flood risk; a "heat-anomaly days" proxy (daily-mean t2m anomaly above +4.5 K for 2 days, labelled *not* an IMD heatwave); and the relative change in 10 m wind power density, never MW. State plainly that humid-heat indices need dew point, which the emulator lacks. Optionally show initial-condition ensemble spread (Gaussian-smoothed noise at about 0.3–0.5× the day-1 RMSE, in ± pairs), labelled "IC-only, underdispersive", and stipple cells where the shock difference is smaller than the spread.

## A 24-hour schedule that banks points early

**Inference.** The order matters more than the ambition. Each step below leaves a submittable repo behind it.

| Hours | Deliverable | Points it secures |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Load .nc, verify units (tp in mm/day), build climatology with `stats.climatology()`, compute persistence/climatology RMSE and CSI on 2019–21 | The baselines every later choice is judged against |
| 2–4 | Damped-anomaly-persistence `serve.py`; clamp t2m/tp; pass `schema_validator` and `repo_scan` locally; first commit with README | Gate, validity, ~11/15 causality, repo 15 |
| 4–10 | Local residual CNN, one-step training, then 2–4-step fine-tune; time 10 steps on 4 threads | RMSE skill, propagation quarter |
| 10–13 | Per-lead anomaly rescaling, tp quantile mapping and CSI threshold tuning, seasonal variance checks | Anti-blur 10, CSI 15 |
| 13–15 | Shock tests with scorer functions (locality, propagation, teleport, null) | Causality 15 |
| 15–21 | Dashboard (painter, triptych, energy–distance plot, wind) and impact cards | Human rounds |
| 21–24 | Two-page brief (design, drift/blur, failure modes; visualisation and impact), final scan, freeze | Brief 15 |

Commit incrementally and honestly throughout, since fabricated history is a disqualification ground in the event rules ([StartupGrantsIndia](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/competitions/the-devforge-hackathon)). Keep weights in the repo (Git LFS is allowed, under 2 GB, per the Track 2 PDF).

## Conclusion

The kit's scorer encodes a clear philosophy: modest, honest skill plus physically plausible behaviour beats raw accuracy. RMSE credit caps at 12% skill. Variance is rewarded only up to a floor. Causality is policed by geometry, not by learned physics. That reverses the usual instinct to reach for the largest model. The decisive design decision is the **per-step receptive field**, which simultaneously guarantees no teleportation, keeps CPU latency trivial and resists overfitting on 17 years of daily data. The decisive engineering decision is to treat blur and heavy rain as **per-lead calibration knobs** tuned on validation, not as losses to be invented under time pressure. Two non-obvious traps deserve the most care. One is the dry-season tp variance ratio, which can zero a whole channel. The other is the repo scanner's string matching on comments and dates.

For the human stages, the differentiator is not a prettier map. It is a dashboard that **shows the scorer's own causality diagnostics live**, paired with an impact module whose every number has a cited definition and a stated caveat. A team that can paint an arbitrary shock in front of judges and watch the break index respond, with the energy-distance curve staying inside the physical speed limit, will have made the "digital twin" claim testable rather than rhetorical. Open questions remain: how SS is averaged across leads and channels, whether demo initial states may ship, and how much skill survives with no boundary information. These are best settled by asking the organisers and by measuring on 2019–21 validation data, not by assumption.
